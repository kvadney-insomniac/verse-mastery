What it is

A closed voice loop, with your hands on the wheel

  • It speaks a reference. You recite the passage from memory.
  • It scores the recitation, tells you how you did, and moves on.
  • No screen interaction at all. Built for the car and the running trail, the two places a memorization app has your attention and cannot have your hands.
  • Originally written for Acts 2 Network Berkeley; much of the app code is theirs, used under MIT. This submission is about the scorer, the ASR architecture, and the AssemblyAI integration.
3
Why not the browser's own recognizer

Three failures nothing tunes around

  • Chrome ends a continuous session silently near 60 seconds, with no error a caller can tell from a real ending. A long passage sits exactly on that line.
  • On Chrome for Android continuous is a documented no-op. The car is a phone, so the mode's whole use case is the one where the flag does nothing.
  • Restarting a session that ended gets rate limited permanently.
Record once, transcribe once. All three disappear, and the wait lands inside a gap the design already had.
4
Application of technology

Using AssemblyAI correctly, not partially

Vocabulary boosting: yes

A server-side word list of proper nouns and archaic forms the engine lacks. Melchizedek, Zerubbabel, thy, steadfast. Passed as word_boost with boost_param: low. Fixes "cannot spell Habakkuk."

Sequence biasing: never

Biasing toward the expected text makes the engine return it whether or not it was said. In a scoring app that is a validity bug: someone who skipped verse 3 would be told they said it.
The route accepts audio and has no field a verse could be put into. A rule enforced by a missing parameter cannot be forgotten by a caller in a hurry.
5
The core insight

Scoring speech is alignment, not comparison

  • A false start is the single most common thing a person does when reciting from memory.
  • Step-wise comparison cannot survive one. Asymmetric gap costs can: omissions expensive, insertions nearly free.
  • Disfluencies become free by construction, not by maintaining a filler-word list that is wrong in a new language the day you ship.
  • Shipped standalone as recital-score, MIT, because the problem is not specific to scripture.
6
Business value

"Did they say the thing" is a question no ASR API answers

Every product below writes the same brittle string comparison we started with, and gets the same 14%.
  • Language learning. Speaking exercises that must not credit a restart as a failure.
  • K-12 reading fluency assessment. Scored aloud, at scale, by people who are not linguists.
  • Pronunciation and speech therapy. Progress measured against a known target.
  • Voice-agent evals. Regression testing whether an agent said what it was supposed to.
Verse Mastery is the reference implementation. recital-score is the product.
7
Engineering

One Worker, two routes, no bundler

  • Static files, React and htm off a CDN. The only server is one Cloudflare Worker, because a browser cannot hold an API key.
  • /api/transcribe is a one-line provider swap: Workers AI, Groq, or AssemblyAI, by environment variable.
  • /api/speak is the agent's voice, Deepgram Aura 2 on the same binding. The browser's own speechSynthesis is a 1990s formant voice, and in a mode whose only output is a voice, that is the product.
  • Both routes are POST-only, capped, and raced against a deadline. An uncontrolled proxy in front of a paid API publishes the key with your billing attached.
  • Client records Opus at 16 kbps: a 40 second recitation is about 80 KB. Hence the pre-recorded API, not the WAV-only sync endpoint.
